The idea. Input (the English students hear and read) gets learners ready, but it isn't enough on its own — they also have to produce language themselves. Producing makes learners notice what they can't yet say, test what they know, and — with practice — say it more and more automatically. The problem: in most classrooms, students hear and read a lot of English but say very little, and a few confident students do nearly all the talking.

So work on two dials at the same time:

Dial 01

Volume

How much each learner says in a class — more turns, more words, more often.

Dial 02

Push

How hard it makes them work — produce carefully and clearly, not just enough to be understood.

Try it tomorrow
  • Use pairs instead of hands-up — everyone answers every question at the same time.
  • Run a 4/3/2: the same talk to a new partner three times, with less time each round (4 → 3 → 2 minutes).
  • Make "say it again, but better" a regular habit between attempts.
Key terms in plain English
Input
the English students hear and read.
Output
the English students say and write.
Pushed output
making students say things carefully and correctly — not just enough to be understood.
Volume
how much each student talks.
Push
how hard the task makes students work with the language.
Fluency
speaking smoothly, without long pauses.
Automatic
the words come without stopping to think.
