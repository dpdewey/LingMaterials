VOCABULARY SET 1 — ECONOMIC MIGRATION & DEVELOPMENT
BRAIN DRAIN (n)
The emigration of educated or skilled people away from a region, leaving it less developed.
"Ayacucho experiences brain drain every year as graduates head to Lima."
URBAN MIGRATION (n)
The movement of people from rural to urban areas in search of opportunity.
"Urban migration is accelerating in the Andes as young people seek professional work."
COMMUNITY INVESTMENT (n)
Using money, time, or skills to develop and improve a local community.
"A doctor who returns home is one of the best forms of community investment."
ENTREPRENEURSHIP (n)
The activity of setting up and running new businesses, often with financial risk.
"Rural entrepreneurship can create local jobs without requiring migration."
TO RETAIN (v)
To keep talented people rather than losing them to another place.
"How can rural provinces retain their most qualified graduates?"
REMITTANCES (n)
Money sent home by people working in another city or country.
"Remittances from Lima support many rural families each month."
ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY (n)
The chance to earn a good wage, advance professionally, or build wealth.
"Students leave because economic opportunities in Lima are simply far greater."
LOCAL TALENT PIPELINE (n)
A system that develops skilled people within a community and keeps them there.
"Our university could become a talent pipeline if salaries were competitive."

INTERACTION TOOLKIT — USE DURING ALL DISCUSSIONS
🗣 TOOLS FOR MANAGING THE CONVERSATION — NOT JUST THE CONTENT

These phrases help you manage the conversation itself — entering, disagreeing, and checking understanding. Try to use at least one in each discussion.

ENTERING / TURN-TAKING
  • "Can I jump in here?"
  • "Sorry to interrupt, but…"
  • "Building on what you said…"
  • "That actually reminds me…"
SOFTENING DISAGREEMENT
  • "I see your point, but…"
  • "I'm not sure I agree — for me…"
  • "That may be true, although…"
  • "I'd push back on that a little…"
CHECKING & REPAIR
  • "Do you mean that…?"
  • "Sorry — are you saying…?"
  • "What I mean is…"
  • "Let me rephrase that…"

"I've been thinking about this a lot lately. I'm from a small town in Junín — my whole family is still there. I finished my degree in Huancayo and then… I don't know. I got a job offer in Lima and I said yes. I told myself it was temporary. That was four years ago. And I think about it all the time. My mom asks when I'm coming back. My little brother is finishing school next year and I know he's going to face the same choice I did. And honestly? I don't know what to tell him. SECTION A — PAIR DISCUSSION
Choose any two questions — discuss with your partner
7 MINUTES
  1. 1Did you face a choice between staying in your hometown and moving somewhere larger? What factors most influenced your decision — or what factors would influence it today?
  2. 2Think of a former student who left the region. Think of one who stayed. Who do you think made the better choice — or is it even fair to compare them?
  3. 3Is there a difference between leaving for economic reasons and leaving for personal ambition? Does the reason change how you feel about it morally?
  4. 4What would need to change in your region for a talented 25-year-old to choose to stay — not out of obligation, but because it genuinely seemed like the best option?
SECTION B — PERSPECTIVE TASK: THE CASE FOR STAYING vs. LEAVING

Your teacher will assign you Group A or Group B. Read your card, identify your 3 strongest arguments. You can create your own unique arguments as well--items not found in the list below. Work with a partner from the other group (arguing the other side) to discuss the issue and make your arguments.

First 4 minutes: argue your assigned position as strongly as you can. Then drop the role and discuss what you actually think.

GROUP A — THE CASE FOR STAYING
  • Rural communities lose their best talent precisely when they need it most
  • Schools, hospitals, and universities in rural areas desperately need qualified staff
  • Lima is already overcrowded — internal migration creates urban poverty, not just rural poverty
  • Entrepreneurship and remote work now make it possible to build a strong career locally
  • Social capital — family networks, trust, deep community ties — is worth more than a higher salary
  • Who will teach the next generation if all the teachers leave?
  • Cultural identity and ancestral knowledge are lost when educated youth become fully urban
Your role: argue strongly for staying. Then tell your partner what you actually think.
GROUP B — THE CASE FOR LEAVING
  • Professional development and career advancement rarely exist in rural areas
  • Salaries in Lima can be 3–4× higher for the same work — this matters for families
  • Young people have the right to pursue the best opportunities available, wherever they are
  • Migrants send remittances home — often greater economic impact than staying on a low local salary
  • Urban experience builds leaders who may later return with more skills, capital, and networks
  • Staying in a limited environment can produce frustration, resentment, and underperformance
  • Brain drain is the government's responsibility to fix — not a burden individuals must carry alone
Your role: argue strongly for leaving. Then tell your partner what you actually think.
