Join the lab

We take students from any major and any year, with or without research experience. This page tells you what joining actually involves so you can decide whether it's for you.

How it works

1

Send an email

Write to ddewey@byu.edu. Say what you're studying, what year you're in, and what caught your interest — a course, a paper, a project on this site. Two paragraphs is plenty.

2

Come to a lab meeting

Sit in on a weekly meeting. You'll see how we talk about data and papers, meet current members, and get a feel for the pace. No preparation needed. Placeholder: day, time, and room.

3

Get trained

New members spend their first few weeks shadowing: learning to fit caps, check signal quality, and run the recording software alongside someone experienced. Most people are running sessions on their own within a semester.

4

Find your project

You'll join an existing study first, then — if you want — develop a question of your own. Students have taken projects to conferences and into co-authored papers.

What we ask of you

Time. Roughly 4–6 hours a week, including lab meeting. More during data collection, less between studies. We'll be honest with you about what a given semester looks like.

Reliability. Participants and teammates count on you showing up when you said you would. This matters more than any technical skill.

Curiosity. You'll be asked what you think. Say it.

Credit or pay. Depending on your program and the semester, work in the lab can count for research credit, mentored-research funding, or an hourly position. Ask.

Not sure you're qualified?

You probably are. Nearly everyone in the lab learned fNIRS here. If you can be curious, careful, and on time, we can teach the rest.

Common questions

I'm a freshman. Is it too early?
No. Earlier is better — you'll have more time to grow into a project.
I've never programmed. Is that a problem?
Not for joining. Some roles involve code and some don't; several members picked up Python here because they wanted to.
Can this count toward a thesis or capstone?
Yes, and it often does. Talk to us early so the project can be shaped for it.
Do I need to be a linguistics major?
No. The lab is housed in Linguistics but roughly half of members come from other departments.
Is there travel?
Sometimes. Students have collected data in Ecuador and presented at conferences in the US and Europe. It isn't required.

Participate in a study

We regularly need participants — language learners, bilinguals, and monolingual English speakers. A session usually takes 45–90 minutes, involves wearing a soft cap while you do a language task or listen to a story, and is completely non-invasive. Compensation varies by study.
