Retrieval practice, on one page.

The cheapest way to make learning last: make students remember things, not just see them again — and spread that remembering over time. Here's the idea, the moves to try tomorrow, and a few checks with instant feedback.

The idea. Forgetting is normal — the brain drops most new information quickly unless we use it again. The strongest fix is also the cheapest: ask students to remember what they learned, instead of just showing it to them again. The act of pulling it out of memory is what makes it stick.

So work on two dials at the same time:

Dial 01

Retrieval

Make students remember it (notes closed), instead of re-reading or re-explaining.

Dial 02

Spacing

Spread the practice over several days, instead of doing it all at once.

Try it tomorrow
  • Start class with a 2-minute brain dump: "Write everything you remember from last time."
  • Ask three quick questions — one from last class, one from last week, one from last month.
  • End class with a no-notes "What did we learn today?" — and don't grade it.
Key terms in plain English
Retrieval
pulling information out of your memory, with notes closed.
Review
looking at the information again (re-reading, re-explaining).
Spacing
spreading practice over several days, not all at once.
Cramming
doing all the practice in one go (also called "massed practice").
Low-stakes
no grade and no pressure — the trying is what matters.
Desirable difficulty
a little difficulty that actually helps learning.
Forgetting curve
how fast we lose new information when we don't use it.
