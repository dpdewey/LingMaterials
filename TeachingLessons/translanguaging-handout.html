Translanguaging  one-page summary/review

Use students' Spanish to build their English — on purpose, with rules. Give Spanish a clear job, and keep English as the final product. Show students you value their thinking--in Spanish--as a tool for prparing to produce strong answers in English.

The idea. "English only" was long a classroom rule not based  research findings. Your students think in Spanish whether you allow it or not, so the smart choice is to use that — with a plan. Let Spanish do the heavy thinking, then have students put their ideas into English. The key word is strategic: Spanish has a clear job, and English is still the goal.

So work on two dials at the same time:

Dial 01

Spanish for thinking

Let the first language do real work — planning, understanding, finding ideas.

Dial 02

English for the product

The final work — said or written — is always in English, and students know it from the start.

Try it tomorrow
  • Let students brainstorm a task in Spanish, then produce the result in English.
  • Before reading a hard English text, let students predict the content in Spanish first.
  • Always name the English product before they start: "You'll present this in English" or "You'll this,   check your predictions against the meaning of the text, ad discuss the match or mismatch in your group."   
Key terms in plain English
Translanguaging
using two languages together, on purpose, for different jobs.
L1
your first language (here, Spanish).
L2
the language you are learning (here, English).
Strategic
planned, with a clear job — not "anything goes" — think strategic use of Spanish.
The product
the final work students say or hand in (here, in English).
Cognitive load
how hard the brain is working at one time.
Idea ceiling
the limit on ideas when students can only say what their English can reach.
