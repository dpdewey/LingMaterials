ESL Professional Survey

TEACH!

A survey about how you think about teaching English — and what you do in your classroom every day. There are no right or wrong answers. Your experience is what matters.

SECTION A What You Believe About Teaching English
A1 In your view, how do students learn a new language? What do you think helps them most — and what gets in the way?

Think about what you have noticed over the years with your own students. There are no correct answers here.

A2 When you think about a student who has truly succeeded in your English class, what does that look like? What does success mean to you — and how does that idea shape your goals as a teacher?

Think about the students you work with most. What would you hope for them?

SECTION B What You Do in the Classroom
B1 Walk us through a typical lesson. What usually happens from the moment students arrive to the end of class?

For example: a warm-up, then presenting something new, then practice, then a freer activity — or whatever your usual pattern is.

B2 When a student makes a mistake in English, what do you usually do? Do you correct right away, wait, or let it go? How do you decide?

You can think about both spoken mistakes and written ones. Do you do anything different depending on the activity?

B3 What kinds of activities or tasks do you use most often to get students speaking or writing in English? Why do those work well for you?

For example: role-plays, pair conversations, games, drills, dictations, project work, songs — whatever you actually use.

B4 How do you check whether your students are making progress? How do you use what you find out to decide what to teach next?

Include both formal tests and informal checks — anything you use to understand where your students are.

B5 How do you help students feel confident and positive about learning English — especially those who feel nervous, embarrassed, or like they "can't do it"?

Think about things you say, activities you choose, or ways you set up the classroom to make students feel safe to try.

B6 How much do you use pair work or group work in your lessons? What do you see as the benefits — and the challenges — of having students practise English with each other rather than only with you?

Think about what actually happens when students work together in your classroom.
