VOCABULARY SET 1 — CIVIC & ELECTORAL PROCESS
RUNOFF (n)
A second election when no candidate wins a majority in the first round.
"Neither candidate got 50%, so there will be a runoff."
COMPULSORY VOTING (n)
A system where citizens are legally required to vote or face a penalty.
"Peru has compulsory voting — you can be fined for not showing up."
POLLING STATION (n)
The place where people go to cast their vote.
"The polling station in my district opened three hours late."
ELECTORAL FRAUD (n)
Illegal actions that affect the outcome of an election.
"Observers found no evidence of electoral fraud despite allegations."
TO ANNUL (v)
To officially declare that a result has no legal force.
"The losing candidate called on authorities to annul the election."
VOTER TURNOUT (n)
The percentage of eligible voters who actually voted.
"Turnout in 2020 was the highest in over a century."
ELECTORAL COLLEGE (n)
A body of electors that formally elects the U.S. president.
"You can win the popular vote but lose the Electoral College."
LOGISTICAL FAILURE (n)
A breakdown in the practical organization of an event.
"Logistical failures delayed hundreds of polling stations."

COMPARISON TASK — PERU & UNITED STATES

Your teacher will assign you either Group A (Peru) or Group B (U.S.). Read your panel carefully — then find a partner from the other group and share what you learned.

🇵🇪 GROUP A — PERU (2026)
  • Two-round system: need >50% of the vote to win outright
  • Compulsory voting for citizens aged 18–70; fines apply for not voting
  • First round: April 12, 2026 — major delays struck Lima polling stations
  • Runoff: Keiko Fujimori (17%) vs. Roberto Sánchez (12%); scheduled June 7
  • Margin between 2nd and 3rd place: only ~21,200 votes (0.09%)
  • Stanford study: delays caused ~27,000 fewer votes — but did not change who advanced
  • JNE (National Jury of Elections) oversees; ONPE administers polling stations
  • Political instability: 9 presidents in the last decade due to congressional impeachment
  • Third-place candidate alleged fraud; election observers found no evidence
🇺🇸 GROUP B — UNITED STATES (2020)
  • Electoral College: need 270 of 538 electoral votes to win — not a direct popular vote
  • Voluntary voting; no legal penalty for not voting
  • 2020 voter turnout: 66.6% — highest since 1900
  • Biden won: 306 electoral votes, 51.3% of the popular vote
  • Record: over 100 million votes cast before Election Day (mail + early voting)
  • It is possible to win the popular vote but lose the election (as in 2016)
  • CISA called 2020 "the most secure election in American history"
  • Trump refused to concede; the January 6, 2021 Capitol attack followed
  • 67+ countries sent election observers; no evidence of systemic fraud found
Discussion — with your partner from the other group
6 MINUTES
  1. 1How does your assigned system decide who wins? Explain it to your partner.
  2. 2What could go wrong in your system? What safeguards exist to prevent or address problems?
  3. 3Which one element of your partner's system would you most want to adopt — or reject? Why?

USEFUL LANGUAGE — EXPRESSING OPINIONS & DISAGREEING POLITELY

Giving Your Opinion

  • "From my perspective, …"
  • "In my experience, …"
  • "I tend to think that …"
  • "What concerns me most is …"
  • "I'm skeptical about … because …"

  • "I see what you mean, but …"
  • "That's a fair point, although …"
  • "I'm not fully convinced that …"
  • "I'd push back on that a little …"
  • "The evidence suggests otherwise …"
