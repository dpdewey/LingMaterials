TEACH FOR SUCCESS! · LESSON 10 OF 24 · CLASS SESSION GUIDE

HOW MUCH IS ENOUGH?

Count/noncount nouns & quantifiers · 120 min · minimal teacher instruction — content is delivered by the study packets

← L9INDEXL11 →
HOW THIS SESSION WORKS: The teacher facilitates but does not lecture. Grammar content is delivered by the study packets (Phase 2), which the TEACH group (Packet A) and the INDIVIDUAL STUDY group (Packet B) complete separately — different rooms or opposite sides of the room, no cross-talk. If any Phase 1–2 instruction is given, give it to each group separately and identically. All later phases are mixed and student-run from this guide.
1REVIEW RETRIEVAL — student-run10 MIN
  • Pairs quiz each other on the 4 items below (from earlier lessons — spaced practice!). Say the FULL sentence aloud, not just the letter.
  • Then: each student asks the partner one exit-ticket question they wrote last lesson.
1. (L9) You ___ see a doctor about that.
  a) should   b) should to
2. (L9) Why don't you ___ her?
  a) call   b) calling
3. (L8) It ___ rain — take an umbrella.
  a) might   b) must to
4. (L7) Heavy bag? I ___ carry it for you!
  a) 'll   b) 'm going to

answers: 1a 2a 3a 4a

Spacing + interleaving: items reach back several lessons, not just the last one. Teacher talk: ~1 min.
2PACKET STUDY — groups A and B, SEPARATELY25 MIN
  • TEACH group: Packet A (L10_packetA). Study to teach — complete every purple PLAN IT box.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: Packet B (L10_packetB). Study for yourself — complete every green MY NOTES box.
  • Groups work in separate spaces. The teacher circulates to keep time and answer procedural (not content) questions.
This phase replaces teacher presentation. Packets are content-identical; only the orientation differs.
3TEACH / CONSOLIDATE12 MIN
  • TEACH group: pair up within the group. Partner 1 teaches the full mini-lesson (explain → examples → discovery question → mini-quiz) for 5 minutes; then swap roles for 5 minutes.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: close the packet. From memory: rewrite the three frames, your two examples, and the answer to the mini-quiz. Then reopen and self-check.
4SORT RACE — mixed pairs (one A + one B)8 MIN
Mark each item: COUNT = A  or  NONCOUNT = B. First pair with all 6 correct wins. Then explain ONE trap item to another pair.
1. ___ homework (a lot of ___)   
2. ___ suggestions (several ___)   
3. ___ luggage (too much ___)   
4. ___ jobs (a few ___)   
5. ___ bread (a little ___)   
6. ___ news (a piece of ___) — trap: it ends in -s!   

answers: 1B 2A 3B 4A 5B 6B

5PARTY SUPPLY RUN — store inventory gap25 MIN
  • Class party, budget 100 soles/dollars. Pairs: one shopper with the needs list (the class brainstorms it first: drinks, snacks, plates… mixing count and noncount), one shopkeeper with a secret inventory card the teacher hands out (quantities and prices differ per shop).
  • Shoppers visit at least three shops. Every purchase requires the full ritual: How much juice do you have? How many cups? Is that enough for forty people? That’s too much — I’ll take two bottles.
  • Shopkeepers may only reveal quantities when asked with the correct much/many — wrong quantifier gets “Sorry, how much WHAT?”
  • Reconvene: which pair fed forty people best under budget? They present their list: We bought three bags of ice, plenty of napkins, but only a little cheese…
6THE FRIDGE DETECTIVE — kitchen memory gap18 MIN
  • Solo (3 min): from memory, list what’s in your real fridge/kitchen right now with honest quantifiers: a lot of eggs, a little milk, no vegetables, too much hot sauce… Minimum eight items.
  • Pairs interrogate each other to reconstruct the partner’s kitchen — only much/many/any questions allowed: Is there any cheese? How much? How many eggs? Draw a quick fridge sketch of what you hear.
  • Compare sketch to list. Score: one point per correctly captured quantity.
  • Close: class votes — whose kitchen is best prepared for a surprise guest, and whose needs an emergency shopping trip?
7ERROR AUCTION — student-run feedback10 MIN
  • During Phases 5–6, each pair writes down two sentences they heard or said (one they think is correct, one they suspect has an error) on slips.
  • Slips go to the front. A volunteer host reads each aloud; teams bet points on CORRECT or FIX IT — a FIX IT bet must include the repair to win.
  • The teacher only referees disputed calls (~2 min of talk).
