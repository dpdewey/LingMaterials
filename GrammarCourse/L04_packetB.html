1LIFE EXPERIENCE — EVER / NEVER / TIMES~6 MIN
Present perfect for experiences at ANY time in life until now — the exact time doesn’t matter. If you then give the specific time or the details, switch to simple past.
Have you ever eaten ceviche? — Yes! I tried it in Lima last year.
I’ve seen that film three times. · She’s never flown.
📝 MY NOTES · Put it in your own words

Explain this rule in your own words. Tip: copy an example into your notes — writing helps memory!

2RECENT NEWS — JUST / ALREADY / YET~6 MIN
Present perfect for recent events that matter now. Just = very recently; already = sooner than expected; yet = until now (questions & negatives, end of sentence).
I’ve just finished my homework. · She’s already left.
Have you eaten yet? — No, I haven’t eaten yet.
📝 MY NOTES · Your own examples

Create TWO true sentences about your own life, then answer for yourself: which part of each sentence shows the pattern?

3BEEN vs GONE + THE -ING COUSIN~5 MIN
Has been to = visited and came back; has gone to = went and is still there. And for actions still in progress, present perfect progressive stresses the ongoing activity: I’ve been studying all morning.
She’s been to Japan twice. · She’s gone to Japan (she’s there now).
My eyes hurt — I’ve been reading for three hours.

5KEY FRAMES — say them out loud~4 MIN

The frames of this lesson — say each one aloud twice:

1  Have you ever ___? — Yes, I have. / No, I never have.
2  I’ve never ___, but I’d like to.
3  I’ve just ___ / I’ve already ___ / I haven’t ___ yet.
