Five things the data suggests

01

Localization/translation is a big door — walk in with eyes open

About one in five postings (17%), but about half of them are freelance, contract, or part-time, and only 69 of its ads state pay (median $93,184). A fine first step; pair it with a specialization.

02

The computing minor is the earnings lever

Computational Linguistics leads every path on pay (median $104,000). Even a few programming and NLP courses move you toward it. Remember these medians span whole careers: senior titles outnumber entry-level ones nearly three to one.

03

Editing and TESOL are dependable professions

Steady demand from many employers: publishers, journals, and companies for editing; colleges, schools, and online programs for TESOL (about half TESOL-fit postings are college, university, or instructor posts). Few ads state a minimum degree, so read each ad's requirements.

04

A second language opens doors

About one in five postings (19%) ask for a specific language; Spanish and French lead, followed by Japanese, German, and Chinese. Many of these are translation and localization jobs, so their stated pay is lower (median $75,460, vs $85,000 for the rest): the language gets you in the door, and other skills raise the pay. Name the language you have on your résumé.

05

Don't overlook government work

Government and intelligence language roles are a small slice (1% of postings), and most of their ads don't state pay. Many are with defense contractors, and about three in four ask for a security clearance — most students never think to search for them.

How to read this honestly

What this snapshot can and can't tell you

Read these limits before quoting a number

  • It's a snapshot, not a census. Postings open during one collection window, not a record of where graduates ended up.
  • The mix reflects where we searched. About two in three postings (65%) came through one commercial aggregator (Adzuna), which adds relatively more Publishing & Professional Editing postings and fewer Localization & Translation and Technology & AI postings than the other sources.
  • These are not starting salaries. The postings mix entry-level, mid-career, and senior positions (448 senior vs 166 entry-level by title), so each median reflects the whole career arc.
  • Pay figures come only from pay the employer stated. Medians use the 732 postings (21%) whose employer stated pay in U.S. dollars. Salary estimates that job sites fill in when an ad gives no pay, and pay in other currencies, are left out (221 postings). How often pay is listed varies by sector — from 11% in Localization & Translation to 38% in Speech & Language Sciences — so treat medians as rough.
  • Remote work is hard to measure. About 20% of postings mention remote or home-based work somewhere, but only 4% list "Remote" as the location, so the true remote share is uncertain.
