1TIME — THE TRIANGLE: AT < ON < IN~6 MIN
Small to big: AT clock times & points (at 7, at noon, at night) → ON days & dates (on Monday, on July 24th, on my birthday) → IN months, seasons, years, parts of day (in July, in winter, in 2026, in the morning). And the silent ones: last/next/every/this take NO preposition.
at 6:30 on Friday in June
✔ See you next week. ✘ on next week
📝 MY NOTES · Put it in your own words

Explain this rule in your own words. Tip: copy an example into your notes — writing helps memory!

2PLACE — POINT, SURFACE, CONTAINER~6 MIN
AT a point/location (at the door, at the bus stop, at work), ON a surface/line (on the table, on the wall, on Main Street), IN an enclosed space or area (in the box, in the kitchen, in Cusco). Arrive: in a city/country, at everything smaller.
She’s at the corner, on the bench, with her feet in the fountain.
We arrived in Lima at ten and at the hotel at eleven.
📝 MY NOTES · Your own examples

Create TWO true sentences about your own life, then answer for yourself: which part of each sentence shows the pattern?

3COLLOCATIONS — THE PREPOSITION BELONGS TO THE WORD~5 MIN
Beyond logic, prepositions travel glued to their verbs and adjectives — learn the pair as ONE word: depend ON, listen TO, wait FOR, ask FOR, agree WITH someone, good/bad AT, afraid OF, interested IN, married TO. Your L1 will whisper the wrong one — the chunk is the antidote.
It depends on the weather. ✘ depends of
I’m waiting for the bus. ✘ waiting the bus
