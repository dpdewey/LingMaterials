STUDY IT — THEN TEACH IT!

Passive voice — present & past · ~25 minutes · You will teach this to a classmate

← L20INDEXL22 →
YOUR MISSION: In a few minutes, you will be the teacher. A classmate will learn this from YOU — your explanation, your examples, your questions. As you study, keep asking: How will I make my student understand this? The purple boxes are your teaching plan.
1THE FLIP — WHEN THE ACTION MATTERS MORE THAN THE ACTOR~6 MIN
Passive = be + past participle. The object steps into the spotlight; the doer retreats or disappears. Use it when the doer is unknown, obvious, or unimportant.
Somebody stole my bike. → My bike was stolen. (who? unknown!)
Rice is grown in the valley. (by farmers — obvious, so unsaid)
🎓 PLAN IT · How will you explain this?

Write the words you will actually SAY to your student. Tip: draw or show an example first — teachers who show are remembered!

2PRESENT vs PAST PASSIVE — ONLY BE CHANGES~6 MIN
The tense lives entirely in BE; the past participle never changes. Present: is/are + made. Past: was/were + made. Agreement with the NEW subject!
Spanish is spoken here. · These shoes are made in Trujillo.
The stadium was opened in 1952. · The photos were taken at dawn.
🎓 PLAN IT · Your teaching examples

Create TWO true sentences about your own life to teach with, then one QUESTION to ask so your student discovers the pattern instead of just hearing it:

3BY-PHRASE — ONLY WHEN THE DOER IS THE NEWS~5 MIN
Add by + doer only if the doer adds real information — inventors, artists, culprits. Passives are everywhere in news, science and museum labels; conversation prefers active, so match the register.
Penicillin was discovered by Fleming. (the by is the point!)
The window was broken by someone. (empty — drop it)
🎓 PLAN IT · Predict your student's mistake

Your student will probably say something like “The bridge built in 1900” — plan a kind, clear correction:

4MINI-QUIZ — test your student!~4 MIN

Solve them yourself FIRST and note the answers small — then give them to your student at the end and check their learning.

Q1 · Flip it: They make these hats in Cajamarca.  my answer: ______________________
Q2 · When would you keep the by-phrase — and why: “This mural was painted (by local students).”  my answer: ______________________
🎓 PLAN IT · Write ONE more quiz item of your own to test your student
5KEY FRAMES & TEACHER TOOLKIT~4 MIN

The frames of this lesson — say each one aloud twice:

1  ___ is/are ___ (made, spoken, grown) ___ .
2  ___ was/were ___ (built, invented, discovered) in ___ .
3  It was ___ by ___ .

Teacher phrases — say each aloud once so they are ready:

"Let me show you something interesting…"
"Look at this sentence. What do you notice?"
"Almost! Remember the pattern — try again."
"Now you try one. Make a sentence about YOUR day."
FINAL CHECK BEFORE YOU TEACH:

I can explain each rule with my own example

I have 2 true examples + 1 discovery question ready

I solved the quiz items and wrote one of my own

I know what to say when my student says “The bridge built in 1900”

Your student’s learning is in your hands. Show before you tell, ask before you explain, and make them produce at least two sentences of their own. Good luck, teacher! 🎓
