THE MOVIE I WATCHED TWICE

Adjective clauses II — object relatives, whose, where · 120 min · minimal teacher instruction — content is delivered by the study packets

← L15INDEXL17 →
HOW THIS SESSION WORKS: The teacher facilitates but does not lecture. Grammar content is delivered by the study packets (Phase 2), which the TEACH group (Packet A) and the INDIVIDUAL STUDY group (Packet B) complete separately — different rooms or opposite sides of the room, no cross-talk. If any Phase 1–2 instruction is given, give it to each group separately and identically. All later phases are mixed and student-run from this guide.
1REVIEW RETRIEVAL — student-run10 MIN
  • Pairs quiz each other on the 4 items below (from earlier lessons — spaced practice!). Say the FULL sentence aloud, not just the letter.
  • Then: each student asks the partner one exit-ticket question they wrote last lesson.
1. (L15) the woman ___ lives next door
  a) who   b) which
2. (L15) the friend who ___ me every day
  a) calls   b) call
3. (L14) She's good ___ chess.
  a) at playing   b) in play
4. (L13) ___ is my favorite exercise.
  a) Swimming   b) Swim

answers: 1a 2a 3a 4a

Spacing + interleaving: items reach back several lessons, not just the last one. Teacher talk: ~1 min.
2PACKET STUDY — groups A and B, SEPARATELY25 MIN
  • TEACH group: Packet A (L16_packetA). Study to teach — complete every purple PLAN IT box.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: Packet B (L16_packetB). Study for yourself — complete every green MY NOTES box.
  • Groups work in separate spaces. The teacher circulates to keep time and answer procedural (not content) questions.
This phase replaces teacher presentation. Packets are content-identical; only the orientation differs.
3TEACH / CONSOLIDATE12 MIN
  • TEACH group: pair up within the group. Partner 1 teaches the full mini-lesson (explain → examples → discovery question → mini-quiz) for 5 minutes; then swap roles for 5 minutes.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: close the packet. From memory: rewrite the three frames, your two examples, and the answer to the mini-quiz. Then reopen and self-check.
4SORT RACE — mixed pairs (one A + one B)8 MIN
Mark each item: PRONOUN CAN DROP = A  or  CANNOT DROP = B. First pair with all 6 correct wins. Then explain ONE trap item to another pair.
1. the movie (that) we saw on Friday   
2. the artist whose murals cover the block   
3. the empanadas (that) my aunt makes   
4. the corner where the accident happened   
5. the advice (that) you gave me   
6. the man who fixed our roof (subject!)   

answers: 1A 2B 3A 4B 5A 6B

5MY FAVORITES MUSEUM — object-relative gap25 MIN
  • Solo (5 min): everyone writes four “museum labels” about their life WITHOUT naming the item — object relatives only: the gift I have kept the longest · the meal my family cooks for every birthday · a song I’ve heard a thousand times · the place where I feel most calm.
  • Gallery walk in pairs: partners exchange labels and interview to uncover each item — minimum three questions per label, answers must reuse the clause: The gift I’ve kept longest is a watch my grandfather gave me.
  • Re-pair. Curate your first partner’s museum for your new partner from memory — two exhibits, full clauses.
  • Close: the class assembles one “Class Museum” label per person on the board — pronoun-drop encouraged, doubled-object banned.
6WHOSE IS IT? — the mystery bag of belongings18 MIN
  • Each student secretly writes one unusual true fact about a possession or a person in their life on a slip: my neighbor’s rooster crows at 2 p.m. sharp · my backpack has survived three countries.
  • Slips into the bag, shuffled, redistributed. Each reader transforms the slip aloud into a whose-clause guess about the room: Someone here has a neighbor whose rooster crows at 2 p.m. Who is the student whose neighbor owns this confused bird?
  • Three guesses per slip; the owner confirms with a full whose-sentence of their own.
  • Scoreboard: correct guesses for readers, best-formed whose-clauses for owners. Class votes the Fact of the Day.
7ERROR AUCTION — student-run feedback10 MIN
  • During Phases 5–6, each pair writes down two sentences they heard or said (one they think is correct, one they suspect has an error) on slips.
  • Slips go to the front. A volunteer host reads each aloud; teams bet points on CORRECT or FIX IT — a FIX IT bet must include the repair to win.
  • The teacher only referees disputed calls (~2 min of talk).
