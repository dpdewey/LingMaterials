HOW THIS SESSION WORKS: The teacher facilitates but does not lecture. Grammar content is delivered by the study packets (Phase 2), which the TEACH group (Packet A) and the INDIVIDUAL STUDY group (Packet B) complete separately — different rooms or opposite sides of the room, no cross-talk. If any Phase 1–2 instruction is given, give it to each group separately and identically. All later phases are mixed and student-run from this guide.
1REVIEW RETRIEVAL — student-run10 MIN
  • Pairs quiz each other on the 4 items below (from earlier lessons — spaced practice!). Say the FULL sentence aloud, not just the letter.
  • Then: each student asks the partner one exit-ticket question they wrote last lesson.
1. (L20) If I ___ rich, I'd buy an island.
  a) were   b) would be
2. (L20) What would you do if you ___ a bear?
  a) saw   b) see
3. (L19) We'll leave when he ___.
  a) arrives   b) will arrive
4. (L18) When the bell rang___ we left.
  a) , (comma)   b) — nothing

answers: 1a 2a 3a 4a

Spacing + interleaving: items reach back several lessons, not just the last one. Teacher talk: ~1 min.
2PACKET STUDY — groups A and B, SEPARATELY25 MIN
  • TEACH group: Packet A (L21_packetA). Study to teach — complete every purple PLAN IT box.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: Packet B (L21_packetB). Study for yourself — complete every green MY NOTES box.
  • Groups work in separate spaces. The teacher circulates to keep time and answer procedural (not content) questions.
This phase replaces teacher presentation. Packets are content-identical; only the orientation differs.
3TEACH / CONSOLIDATE12 MIN
  • TEACH group: pair up within the group. Partner 1 teaches the full mini-lesson (explain → examples → discovery question → mini-quiz) for 5 minutes; then swap roles for 5 minutes.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: close the packet. From memory: rewrite the three frames, your two examples, and the answer to the mini-quiz. Then reopen and self-check.
4SORT RACE — mixed pairs (one A + one B)8 MIN
Mark each item: KEEP THE BY-PHRASE = A  or  DROP IT = B. First pair with all 6 correct wins. Then explain ONE trap item to another pair.
1. Hamlet was written ___ (by Shakespeare).   
2. My car was stolen ___ (by somebody) last night.   
3. The goal was scored ___ (by the youngest player in history).   
4. English is spoken ___ (by people) in the office.   
5. The mural was painted ___ (by three local grandmothers).   
6. The road is being repaired ___ (by workers).   

answers: 1A 2B 3A 4B 5A 6B

5THE INVENTION AUCTION — quiz gap22 MIN
  • Pairs: one quizmaster with the answer card (ten facts the teacher provides: inventions, discoveries, constructions — telephone / 1876 / Bell; Machu Picchu / 15th century…), one contestant with only the item names.
  • Contestants must EARN each fact by asking in passive: When was the telephone invented? Who was it invented by? Where were these built? — active questions get a cheerful “Rephrase, please!”
  • Every earned fact must be banked as a full passive sentence in the contestant’s notes: The telephone was invented by Bell in 1876.
  • Swap roles with a new card. Team with the most correctly banked sentences reads their three favorites as museum labels — dramatic voice required.
6BREAKING NEWS — the newsroom transformation22 MIN
  • News teams of 3 receive (or invent) five chaotic eyewitness reports in active voice: Somebody painted the statue purple last night! Workers found a 200-year-old letter inside the wall!
  • The editor’s rule: headlines and openings in passive, doers only where they’re the story: STATUE PAINTED PURPLE OVERNIGHT — a 200-year-old letter was found during repairs…
  • Each team drafts a 60-second news bulletin: three passive headlines + one developed story with at least one by-phrase that earns its place.
  • Bulletins are performed newsreader-style; the audience fact-checks the grammar and votes for Best Newsroom. Blooper rule: an accidental ‘was happened’ costs a point — happen never flips!
7ERROR AUCTION — student-run feedback10 MIN
  • During Phases 5–6, each pair writes down two sentences they heard or said (one they think is correct, one they suspect has an error) on slips.
  • Slips go to the front. A volunteer host reads each aloud; teams bet points on CORRECT or FIX IT — a FIX IT bet must include the repair to win.
  • The teacher only referees disputed calls (~2 min of talk).
8EXIT TICKET & HOMEWORK LAUNCH10 MIN
From memory — no notes:

1. One present passive about your country (something is made/grown/spoken…):

2. One past passive about your city (something was built/opened/founded…):

3. One by-phrase that truly earns its place:

  • Open the L21 Practice Module (~2 hours, in parts). Do parts A–F before next class; save COME BACK for two days later.
  • Don’t skip the SHARE task — you will read your report to a partner at the start of the next lesson.
TIME TOTAL: ~120 min · Teacher talk: under 15 min · Everything else is pair/group interaction.
