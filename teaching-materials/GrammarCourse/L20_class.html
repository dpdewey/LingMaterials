TEACH FOR SUCCESS! · LESSON 20 OF 24 · CLASS SESSION GUIDE

IF I WON THE LOTTERY

Unreal conditionals — the second conditional · 120 min · minimal teacher instruction — content is delivered by the study packets

← L19INDEXL21 →
HOW THIS SESSION WORKS: The teacher facilitates but does not lecture. Grammar content is delivered by the study packets (Phase 2), which the TEACH group (Packet A) and the INDIVIDUAL STUDY group (Packet B) complete separately — different rooms or opposite sides of the room, no cross-talk. If any Phase 1–2 instruction is given, give it to each group separately and identically. All later phases are mixed and student-run from this guide.
1REVIEW RETRIEVAL — student-run10 MIN
  • Pairs quiz each other on the 4 items below (from earlier lessons — spaced practice!). Say the FULL sentence aloud, not just the letter.
  • Then: each student asks the partner one exit-ticket question they wrote last lesson.
1. (L19) If it ___, we'll stay home.
  a) rains   b) will rain
2. (L19) ___ you hurry, you'll miss it.
  a) Unless   b) If
3. (L18) cheap; ___, it broke in a week.
  a) however   b) but
4. (L17) Before you go___ close the window.
  a) , (comma)   b) ; (semicolon)

answers: 1a 2a 3a 4a

Spacing + interleaving: items reach back several lessons, not just the last one. Teacher talk: ~1 min.
2PACKET STUDY — groups A and B, SEPARATELY25 MIN
  • TEACH group: Packet A (L20_packetA). Study to teach — complete every purple PLAN IT box.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: Packet B (L20_packetB). Study for yourself — complete every green MY NOTES box.
  • Groups work in separate spaces. The teacher circulates to keep time and answer procedural (not content) questions.
This phase replaces teacher presentation. Packets are content-identical; only the orientation differs.
3TEACH / CONSOLIDATE12 MIN
  • TEACH group: pair up within the group. Partner 1 teaches the full mini-lesson (explain → examples → discovery question → mini-quiz) for 5 minutes; then swap roles for 5 minutes.
  • INDIVIDUAL STUDY group: close the packet. From memory: rewrite the three frames, your two examples, and the answer to the mini-quiz. Then reopen and self-check.
4SORT RACE — mixed pairs (one A + one B)8 MIN
Mark each item: REAL (FIRST) = A  or  UNREAL (SECOND) = B. First pair with all 6 correct wins. Then explain ONE trap item to another pair.
1. If I see him tomorrow, I’ll return the book.   
2. If I saw a ghost, I would faint immediately.   
3. If it’s sunny on Sunday, we’ll hike.   
4. If dogs could talk, mine would never stop.   
5. If I were you, I’d take the offer.   
6. If the bus comes late again, I’ll complain.   

answers: 1A 2B 3A 4B 5B 6A

5HYPOTHETICAL HOT SEAT22 MIN
  • Groups of 4–5 build a Question Jar: each student contributes two What would you do if…? slips (imaginative but answerable: …if you woke up speaking perfect English? …if you found a door in your house you’d never seen?).
  • One member takes the hot seat for three questions drawn from the jar; answers must be full second conditionals PLUS one follow-up chain from the group (And if that happened, what would you do next?).
  • Hesitation over five seconds or a broken structure passes the question — the group supplies the correction chorally before moving on.
  • Rotate until everyone has sat. Each group sends its single best question to the class jar for Lesson 21’s warm-up.
6DESERT ISLAND COUNCIL — the ranking gap22 MIN
  • The ship is sinking (calmly, hypothetically). Pairs receive different halves of a ten-item salvage list (A: rope, mirror, chocolate, radio, tarp; B: knife, matches, novel, pot, first-aid kit).
  • Dictate lists to each other, then negotiate the TOP FIVE — every argument in unreal grammar: If we took the mirror, we could signal planes. If we didn’t have the pot, we wouldn’t boil water.
  • Merge into fours; the two pairs must defend their rankings and settle one final list. Disagreements are resolved by what-would-happen-if chains, not volume.
  • Councils present their five with one justification each; the class votes on which island team survives longest.
7ERROR AUCTION — student-run feedback10 MIN
  • During Phases 5–6, each pair writes down two sentences they heard or said (one they think is correct, one they suspect has an error) on slips.
  • Slips go to the front. A volunteer host reads each aloud; teams bet points on CORRECT or FIX IT — a FIX IT bet must include the repair to win.
  • The teacher only referees disputed calls (~2 min of talk).
