TEACH FOR SUCCESS! · LESSON PLAN (teacher)

FOOTBALL & US

Football & Us · CEFR B1–B2 · 65 min

Upper-intermediate65 min · 55-min trimTeacher talk < 1/3

Why this lesson

Millions of people love football. But why do we care so much about a game with a ball? It is really about belonging, feelings, and being part of something bigger.

MY NOTE — personal reminders and in-the-moment cuesThis is the upper-intermediate edition. Vocabulary and prompts are simpler than the advanced version; it’s anticipated that students will speak more freely when the language load is lighter, so plan to step back and let pairs run.
1Warm-up: scan the survey5 minTTT low

Hand out (or display) the opinion survey. Students read the statements quietly and tick their first reactions. No talking yet — this primes the topic and the target vocabulary.

MY NOTE — personal reminders and in-the-moment cuesIt’s anticipated that a few words will be new here; that’s fine. Tell students they will learn them next, so they should just guess for now.
2Vocabulary input (two sets)10 minTTT med

Set 1 — core words:

teamnoun
a group of players who play together
fannoun
a person who loves a team or sport
matchnoun
a game between two teams
scoreverb/noun
to get a goal / the number of goals
winverb
to be the best in a game
loseverb
to not win a game

Set 2 — build on it:

supportverb
to follow and help a team
proudadj
feeling happy about something you or your team did
celebrateverb
to show you are happy after success
defeatnoun/verb
a loss / to beat another team
rivalnoun
a team or person you always compete against
competeverb
to try to win against others
MY NOTE — personal reminders and in-the-moment cuesStudents will likely already know one or two of these. Drill only the trickier items chorally, then move on — don’t over-explain easy words.
3Video / input stimulus8 minTTT low

A short clip of fans celebrating a goal, or a national team singing before a match. Play it twice. First time: just watch. Second time: students note one thing they see and one thing they feel.

No video? Read-aloud fallback

Read aloud: “When the ball goes into the net, thousands of people jump up together. Strangers hug each other. For 90 minutes, they are not strangers — they are one team, one city, one country.”

4Pair discussion + Interaction Toolkit10 minTTT low

Pairs discuss the questions below. The toolkit gives them ready phrases so they keep talking instead of stopping to translate.

  1. Do you like football? Why or why not?
  2. Is there a team or a sport that you support? Tell your partner about it.
  3. How do you feel when a team you like wins? And when it loses?
  4. Do many people in your country watch football? When?

USEFUL LANGUAGE: TALKING ABOUT SPORT & FEELINGS

  • I support… because…
  • When they win, I feel…
  • I’m not really into…, but…
  • The best thing about… is…
  • I agree up to a point, but…
MY NOTE — personal reminders and in-the-moment cuesIt’s anticipated that early finishers will go quiet. Have a follow-up ready: ‘Tell me one more reason.’ Circulate and collect errors for the next phase.
5Language Clinic 15 minTTT med

Anticipated focus: present simple for habits and routines (I watch every weekend; they play on Sundays). It’s likely some students will drop the third-person ‘s’ (he play → he plays).

Put 3–4 real student sentences on the board (no names). The class fixes them together.

6Jigsaw: different points of view10 minTTT low

Question for the group: Is winning the most important thing in sport?

Give each student one role card. They read it, then explain that person’s opinion to the group in their own words.

A PLAYERI train hard every day. For me, winning is everything.
A FANI just love watching with my friends. The result is not so important.
A COACHI want my team to play well and respect the other team, win or lose.
A PARENTI want my children to have fun and stay healthy, not only to win.
7Likert debate8 minTTT low

Read each statement. Students move or raise hands to show agree / not sure / disagree, then defend their choice with one reason.

Football is more than just a game.
Winning is the most important thing in sport.
Football brings the people of a country together.
Top players are paid too much money.
It is okay to feel very sad when your team loses.
8Language Clinic 24 minTTT med

Anticipated focus: adjectives of feeling vs verbs (I’m excited / it excites me; I’m disappointed / it disappoints me). Students will probably mix ‘bored’ and ‘boring’.

9Output task (choose one)7 minTTT low

Choose ONE way to share your ideas about football:

  • Describe a match or a sports moment you will never forget.
  • Write about your favourite player or team, and say why you like them.
  • Design your own team: a name, colours, and one rule for the players.
MY NOTE — personal reminders and in-the-moment cuesFor the 55-minute trim: set the output task as homework and end after the debate.
10One-word close2 minTTT low

Go round the room: Say one word for how football (or sport) makes you feel. No explaining — just one word each.
