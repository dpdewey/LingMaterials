Make speaking do more work

Pushed output gives every learner a reason to say more, notice a gap, and try again with greater clarity, precision, or detail. Use this page to plan one instructor-initiated activity and check the design.

The idea. Input (the English students hear and read) prepares learners, but output gives them a chance to use the language. When learners try to express a meaning, they may notice a gap, test a form, receive feedback, and try again. That work can support development in accuracy, complexity, fluency, and access to language.

Important qualification. Pushed output is not simply “more talking,” and output alone does not guarantee acquisition. The strongest tasks combine a meaningful message with a reason every learner must speak, a manageable push, and a chance to reformulate.

So work on two dials at the same time:

Dial 01

Participation

How much each learner says — more turns, more words, and more opportunities to retrieve language.

Dial 02

Productive challenge

How much the task asks learners to clarify, extend, compare, be precise, or say it again better.

Try it tomorrow
  • Picture story + reformulation: retell, then “say it again” with a connector, precise verb, or extra detail.
  • Information-gap troubleshooting: ask targeted questions to solve a problem neither partner can solve alone.
  • Describe-and-draw: describe a hidden image; the listener asks for clarification and checks the result.
  • Three-round story repetition: repeat the same story to new partners with less time each round.
  • Classroom decision challenge: choose a plan, give reasons, compare options, and defend one decision.
The learning cycle

In practice, a useful push keeps the learner responsible for the language. The instructor sets the purpose, listens for a productive next step, and prompts the learner to continue or reformulate.

01 · Meaning

Say something real

Complete a story, solve a problem, describe a picture, or make a decision.

02 · Notice

Find the gap

What word, form, connector, or level of detail is missing?

03 · Push

Try a little more

Ask for precision, explanation, comparison, or an additional detail.

04 · Rebuild

Say it again

Reformulate, extend, or retrieve the language in a new attempt.

Key terms in plain English
Input
the English students hear and read.
Output
the English students say and write.
Pushed output
meaningful production that asks learners to clarify, extend, notice, or reformulate their language.
Noticing a gap
recognizing that the language you can produce is not yet precise, complete, or effective enough.
Reformulation
saying the message again in a clearer, more accurate, more complex, or more detailed way.
Fluency
speaking smoothly, without long pauses.
Automaticity
accessing useful language with less conscious effort.
Prompt bank: keep the learner talking

Use a brief prompt before supplying an answer. Choose one that matches the goal of the task.

  • Can you say more?
  • What happened next?
  • Can you be more precise?
  • Can you compare them?
  • Try that again with a past-tense verb.
  • Say it again, but better.
